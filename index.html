Why Jefferson

Four services. One standard.

What did he do in the past that earns your trust? He wrote it. He fought for it. He defended it abroad. He served it, and walked away when it was abused.

I
1776The Founding Pen

Author of the Declaration of Independence

The Fact

In 1776, Jefferson drafted the Declaration, which expressed the concepts of natural rights, liberty, and government based on the consent of the governed.

Why It Matters to You

This appeals to voters because he helped shape the principles America was founded on. The man who proclaimed America free will speak up and defend that freedom from excessive government power.

Every right now under attack, to speak and to dissent, he put in writing before there was a Congress capable of violating them.

He wrote the standard, and he will hold the government accountable to it.

II
1777–17869 Years Fought

Author of the Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom

The Fact

Jefferson drafted the Statute in 1777 and fought for nine years until it passed in 1786. It declared that no man should be forced to support a ministry he does not believe in, that none should suffer on account of their opinions, and that truth needs no help from force to prevail.

Why It Matters to You

This shows Jefferson has already fought this battle and won. A man who placed belief above the reach of government will not allow the government to jail a printer for his beliefs.

The Federalists argue that a republic cannot stand citizens' disagreement. Virginia has survived it for fourteen years, and needed no sedition law.

Jefferson has already answered the argument the Federalists are making.

III
1785–17894 Years · 0 Wars

Minister to France

The Fact

Jefferson was the U.S. Minister to France from 1785 to 1789. He worked on behalf of Americans in Europe and learned firsthand how European politics and diplomacy operate.

Why It Matters to You

This builds Jefferson's credibility because voters can see him as someone who protects American interests without unnecessarily resorting to military expansion, which is how power shifts away from the people's liberties.

He secured American standing abroad for four years without war, and gained more with diplomacy than the Federalists could ever gain with a war.

IV
1790–1793Resigned on Principle

First Secretary of State

The Fact

From 1790 to 1793 Jefferson served as the nation's first Secretary of State under President George Washington. He played a major role in forming American foreign policy and served directly inside the new federal government.

Why It Matters to You

Jefferson is not only an idealist. He has managed national affairs, and he knows how the federal government should function and how it can be tested. When he judged it was being bent toward the interests of a few, he resigned rather than serve it.

He has seen what this government can become, and that is why he means to limit it. Ask what it means that Jefferson seeks the presidency in order to weaken it.

Every candidate promises well, but only Jefferson promises to hold less power, and he is the only one who has proven he will walk away from it.

That is what he has done. Here is what he will do.

The Platform

Two promises. Both cost him power.

Smaller government at home. Open rivers in the West. A republic of free men and free farms.

I.
Goal: Limited Government

Restore the Natural Liberties of the People

Less Government. Lower Taxes. Free Speech.

Jefferson supports the natural liberties of the people and individual freedoms, which means reducing the size and scope of the federal government. He wants a limited federal government, lower taxes, and a smaller military to protect individual liberties and states' rights.

The Alien and Sedition Acts are the clearest proof of why this is necessary. They made criticism of the government a crime, and printers and editors went to prison for it. A law that punishes criticism does not protect the republic. It only protects those who run it.

Jefferson opposed the Acts from their passage, writing the Kentucky Resolutions of 1798 to argue that a federal law exceeding the Constitution requires no citizen to obey it.

A government that fears your opinions has admitted it cannot answer them.

II.
Goal: An Empire of Liberty

Open the West. Free the Mississippi.

Open Land. Open Rivers. Open Markets.

Jefferson envisions an empire of liberty supported by the agricultural roots of society. To achieve it, he will expand west for land and trade routes. He intends to secure navigation of the Mississippi and the port of New Orleans, and to open the western lands to settlement.

Every bushel grown west of the mountains must pass through one port.

Open rivers and open land mean that what a man grows and earns, he can sell. Spain holds that port today, and every farmer west of it sells under a foreign power's control. Until America has full control over its own trade, it remains a country dependent on others.

They offer you a fleet. He offers you a farm.

Every promise on this page costs the president power. That is how you know he means to keep them.

Now compare it to the man who already holds that power, and what he has done with it.

The Adams Record

Two charges. One pattern.

He signed a declaration against a tyrant king. Then he took the king's habits for his own. Here is the evidence.

Record I: The Sedition Act

Signed 1798
Guilty
The Charge

John Adams signed the Sedition Act, making it a federal crime to publish "scandalous" and "malicious" writing against the President or Congress. It led to the arrest of newspaper editors and ordinary citizens: an abuse of the First Amendment and an abuse of federal power.

The Evidence
  • Within months · 1798
    Congressman Matthew Lyon of Vermont, imprisoned

    Jailed for publishing criticism of John Adams and the federal government, including the charge that Adams had

    "an unbounded thirst for ridiculous pomp, foolish adulation, and selfish avarice."
  • 1798
    Benjamin Franklin Bache, printer, arrested

    Arrested for something he printed. He died of yellow fever before he ever stood trial.

  • 1798
    Luther Baldwin, a man in a tavern

    He wrote nothing. One crude joke in a Newark tavern, and the federal government came for him.

  • This year · 1800
    James Callender, imprisoned

    Jailed for a pamphlet written against the President.

The Attack
Printers jailed.Editors arrested.Congressmen imprisoned.

This is not punishing the traitors in America. It is punishing the critics of John Adams.

John Adams signed the Declaration of Independence, a document that at its heart is a published list of grievances against a head of state. In 1776 he put his name to an indictment of a king. How strange, then, that he has made criticizing himself a crime. He didn't change his principles. He changed which side he stands on, depending on his situation.

Take a close look at the law and its purpose is plain. It protects the President. It protects Congress. But it does not protect the Vice President, Thomas Jefferson, who just so happens to be his political rival. Isn't it funny that Adams may not be insulted, but his rival can be slandered freely?

If the law truly existed to defend the "government's honor," the second-highest officer would be inside it. He isn't, because the Vice President is Thomas Jefferson, leader of the opposition and the man running against Adams. Federalist papers can freely call Jefferson an atheist, while a Republican paper that answers risks imprisonment. One candidate can be attacked. The other cannot strike back.

Who decides what is "false"? A Federalist prosecutor, in front of a Federalist judge, often with a jury chosen by Federalist officials. A court should be the one place a citizen can stand equal to his government. Under Adams, it has become the place where the government punishes him for speaking.

IV.Read what it does to you.

Don't think you are safe just because you own no press. Luther Baldwin wrote nothing. He made one crude joke in a Newark tavern, and the federal government came for him.

You do not need to be jailed to be silenced. Watching your neighbor be jailed is enough. The next time you complain about an unjust tax, you will quiet down. That is exactly how the law is meant to work: it imprisons a few so that everyone else goes quiet. This law gives no power and no freedom to the people. Perhaps its purpose is to establish a tyranny in which the people can do nothing at all.

V.Why this matters to you.

Your vote is only as good as what you know when you cast it. If the only papers left are the ones that praise Adams, you will walk into this election knowing only the narrow view he allows you to know.

And before you dismiss this, know that he had the power to refuse this law, and he didn't. Isn't it funny that the same man asking for your trust believes your words require his permission?

Record II: The Army & the House Tax

1798–1799
Guilty
The Charge

During the Quasi-War with France in 1798, Adams supported legislation expanding the military and signed the law creating the Department of the Navy, and the Federalist Congress raised an army. To pay for it, he passed a $2 million direct tax on houses, land, and slaves. When Pennsylvania farmers resisted in 1799, he ordered federal troops to march against them.

The Evidence
  • 1798
    Department of the Navy created

    Signed into law by Adams. The Federalist Congress raises an army against France.

  • 1798
    $2,000,000 direct tax

    Laid on houses, land, and slaves to pay for the military buildup.

  • 1799
    Troops march on Pennsylvania

    Farmers resist the tax. Adams sends federal soldiers against them.

The Attack
Raised against France.Marched against farmers.

The army was raised against France, yet no French soldier ever set foot on American soil. The only people it marched against were American farmers, understandably angry about a tax. Adams's army, built to "defend you," is being used directly against you.

Why It's Bad

I.A militia goes home. A standing army does not.

In 1776, in the Declaration of Independence that John Adams signed, I listed among King George's many crimes that he "kept among us, in times of peace, Standing Armies." I wrote it as an accusation, a thing we must never follow. The Federalists seem to have taken it as an instruction.

Why should you fear a standing army in times of peace? It is not like a militia, made up of your neighbors, who go back to their farms and families when the fighting stops. A standing army is different. The government pays it, feeds it, and decides its future, so its loyalty lies with the government rather than the people.

Consider the record of John Adams, who puts his critics in jail. In his hands, a standing army becomes very dangerous and gives him enormous power. If he is willing to imprison people for speaking their minds, what stops him from continuing his wrongdoing with soldiers?

II.And you are paying for it.

Government assessors entered people's homes and counted their windows to decide what they owed.

Think about who is paying for the army. It's not merchants. It's not bankers. It is you. Most farmers own land but have very little money, so this tax forces them to pay cash they don't have for land they can't easily sell.

Your home should be the one private, safe place where the government cannot follow. Adams sent his collectors through it.

III.Each step builds on the last.

First,the Federalists built the army.
Thenthey taxed your home to sustain the army.
Thenthey jailed the people who spoke against it.

Each step makes control easier and resistance harder. A government that can build its own army, pay for it with your money, and silence your words doesn't need your permission anymore. The government runs on its own.

Your precious vote can still change this. Vote carefully.

A Final Warning

Fellow Citizens,

I have shown you two records. Now let me tell you what they mean when you hold them side by side.

The first record took your power: your voice. Under John Adams, a printer can be jailed for an article, a congressman for a letter, and a man in a tavern for a joke. The second record took your home and your safety. Under John Adams, a tax collector counts your windows to pay for an army that no enemy ever came to fight, an army that marched only against farmers like you.

Your voice is the power that lets you defend everything else. If a tax is unjust, your voice is how you fight it. If the army is turned on you, your voice is how you warn your neighbors. Your voice is what lets free people correct their government peacefully. Take it away, and only two choices remain: silence or rebellion. Pennsylvania's farmers chose rebellion and were met with soldiers. Adams now believes the rest of you will choose silence.

Do not give him that belief. Think about what a vote for Adams actually approves.

It says that jailing printers is acceptable.
It tells him the army was worth your house.
It tells him that the next time you object, he is cleared to answer with prison.
It tells him he is forgiven, and that what he has done is acceptable.

A vote is not only a choice of who leads. It is also a judgment of what he did. Do not think of another four years as a second chance. Think of it as permission.

Think about what you are trading. He offers safety from a French invasion that never happened. The price is your voice, your home, and your peace of mind. No threat from abroad ever cost you anywhere near as much as he has. It began with the foreigners, then the printers, then the farmers, and next, perhaps, you.

This November you still have your voice. Use it while you can.

Thomas Jefferson
